# Why do we need cryptography?

*Week 1 · Essay 1 · October 19, 2026*

There is a lot of information that is used in our daily lives, such as identity numbers, credit card details and passwords. Most of this information is digital. When people want to share this information, they use digital apps. People don't have to know how this information is sent or received, but they should know whether it is safe.

We all know confidentiality is important, but it's more important than we think. For instance, unless we share our sensitive data, such as our bank password, it should stay private. This is where we need cryptography. One question can come to mind: what does cryptography do differently? Cryptography uses mathematical functions to keep our private information private. So this information is not only stored in databases; it is also encrypted.

Cryptography also protects integrity. In a blockchain, every block contains the hash of the previous block, and every transaction is digitally signed. If someone changes an old transaction, its hash changes and everyone on the network can see it. As a result, changing the record without being noticed is practically impossible.

Identity is used in different situations, for example, when we log in to a banking app. In this situation, people must give evidence about their identity and make sure that others are who they claim to be. Cryptography can make a unique digital signature, so it can prove an identity.

In conclusion, our information needs protection whenever we share it. Cryptography helps by using its unique mathematical functions to do that job. It provides confidentiality, authentication and integrity.
